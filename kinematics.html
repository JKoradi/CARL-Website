Two motors on one shaft axis drive L₁ and L₂. L₃ and L₄ are passive; L₄ is a single rigid body with a fixed kink κ at its pin to L₂, and the foot sits at the end of L₄.₂.

Given Foot target, measured from the shaft axis F = (x, y), y positive downward
Step 1 Kink joint K — circle ∩ circle |K − B| = L₂ and |K − F| = L₄.₂
θ₂ = atan2(Kx−Bx, Ky)
Step 2 Orientation of the rigid L₄ body ψ = ∠(K→F) rotated back by 180°−κ
Step 3 Far pin E, where L₄.₁ meets L₃ E = K + L₄.₁ · û(ψ)
Step 4 Reach check on the L₁–L₃ pair r = |E − A|, need |L₁−L₃| ≤ r ≤ L₁+L₃
Step 5 Elbow P₁ — circle ∩ circle |P₁ − A| = L₁ and |P₁ − E| = L₃
Result Shaft angles you command θ₁ = atan2(P₁x−Ax, P₁y)
θ₂ from step 1 — both zero pointing straight down, positive toward +x

No iteration anywhere: the foot fixes K by two circles, K and κ fix the whole L₄ body, and E then fixes P₁ by two more circles. Each intersection has two roots — the flip buttons pick the other assembly mode. With L₁ = L₄.₁ and L₂ = L₃ the loop is a parallelogram, so L₄ stays parallel to L₁ and the foot reduces to F = L₂·û(θ₂) + L₄.₂·û(θ₁+180°−κ).
